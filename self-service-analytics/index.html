Self-Service Analytics for Business Users

⏱️ 60–90 minutes🎯 Level: Beginner — no technical background needed💻 Just a web browser

Welcome! In this workshop you'll learn how to get answers from your company's data by simply asking questions in plain English — no spreadsheets to wrangle, no dashboards to hunt through, and absolutely no code.

You'll work with Ana, TextQL's AI data analyst. Think of Ana as a skilled analyst who sits next to you, has already studied your company's data, and is available 24/7. You ask a question the way you'd ask a colleague; Ana does the technical work and brings back the answer — with her work shown.

What you'll learn

  • Module 1: Ask your first question and get a real answer
  • Module 2: Refine answers with follow-up questions, like a conversation
  • Module 3: Turn answers into charts and visuals
  • Module 4: Understand why you can trust the numbers
  • Module 5: Save your work and share it with your team
  • Module 6: Patterns for asking great questions (the cheat sheet)

Who this is for

Anyone who needs answers from data to do their job: operations, finance, marketing, sales, customer service, HR, leadership. If you can write an email, you can do everything in this workshop.

Two ways to run this workshop

Self-paced: work through it on your own, top to bottom — every step tells you exactly what to type.
Guided: a TextQL team member or your internal champion leads the group, and you follow along hands-on.

A note on the examples

The example questions in this workshop use everyday business terms like orders, customers, revenue, and region. Your data has its own vocabulary — swap in the metrics and categories your team actually uses. The patterns work the same.

Meet Ana, Your Data Analyst

⏱️ 5 minutes · reading only

Before you type anything, it helps to know what's actually happening on the other side of the chat — in plain terms.

What Ana does with your question

When you ask something like “How did sales do last quarter?”, Ana:

  1. Figures out what you mean. She knows your company's definitions — what counts as a “sale,” when your fiscal quarter starts, which system is the source of truth.
  2. Does the analyst work. She finds the right data, writes and runs the technical queries, and double-checks the result. You'll see her narrate her steps as she goes.
  3. Answers in plain English. You get the number, the context, and often a chart — plus the full detail of how she got it, if you ever want to look.

Why her answers are consistent

Behind the scenes, your organization maintains something called an ontology — think of it as your company's official dictionary for data. It records, for example, exactly what “revenue” means, how “active customer” is calculated, and which numbers are the governed, agreed-upon versions. Ana always answers from that dictionary, which means:

  • You and your colleague asking the same question get the same answer.
  • The answer matches what finance or analytics would have produced by hand.
  • When a definition changes, it changes in one place — and everyone's answers update with it.

You'll see this in action in Module 4: Can I Trust This Number?

Three habits that make this easy

HabitWhy it helps
Ask like you'd ask a person.Full, natural questions work better than keyword fragments. “Which products grew fastest this year?” beats “product growth.”
It's a conversation, not a search box.Don't try to cram everything into one perfect question. Ask, look, then refine: “now just the West region,” “compare to last year.”
When unsure, ask Ana about the data itself.“What data do you have about customers?” is a perfectly good question — and a great way to discover what's possible.

Module 1 · Ask Your First Question

⏱️ 10 minutes🎯 Goal: get a real answer from your data

Step 1 — See what's available

Start by asking Ana what she can work with. This is the best icebreaker — it tells you what's possible before you ask anything specific.

TYPE THIS TO ANA
What data do you have access to? Give me a quick plain-English tour of what I could ask about.
What you'll see

Ana describes the connected data areas in business terms — for example sales, customers, products, support tickets — rather than technical table names. Pick the area most relevant to your job; you'll use it for the rest of the workshop.

Step 2 — Ask your first real question

Now ask one simple, concrete question about a number you care about. Use one of these as a pattern and swap in your own terms:

TYPE THIS TO ANA (PICK ONE & ADAPT)
How many orders did we have last month?
OR
What was our total revenue last quarter?
OR
How many new customers did we add this year?

Step 3 — Watch Ana work

Don't skip this part — it's the most important 30 seconds of the workshop. While Ana works, she narrates her steps: what she's looking at, what she's checking, what she found. This isn't a black box spitting out a number; it's an analyst showing her work.

What you'll see

A short series of working steps, then a plain-English answer — e.g. “You had 12,408 orders in May, up 6% from April.” — often with a small table or chart, and an expandable view of exactly how it was calculated.

If Ana asks you a question back

That's a good sign, not a problem. If your question is ambiguous (“sales” could mean bookings or invoiced revenue), Ana asks which one you mean instead of guessing. Just answer her like you would a colleague.

Module 2 · Make It a Conversation

⏱️ 15 minutes🎯 Goal: refine an answer with follow-ups instead of new searches

Here's the mindset shift that makes self-service analytics click: you don't need the perfect question. Ana remembers everything in the current thread, so you can start broad and steer — exactly like talking to an analyst at your desk.

Stay in the same chat from Module 1 and run these follow-ups in order (adapting the words to your data):

Step 1 — Break it down

TYPE THIS TO ANA
Break that down by region.
What you'll see

The same number, split into a small table — notice you didn't have to repeat the question, the time period, or anything else. “That” was enough.

Step 2 — Narrow it

TYPE THIS TO ANA
Just show the top 5, and add what % of the total each one represents.

Step 3 — Compare over time

TYPE THIS TO ANA
How does that compare to the same period last year?
What you'll see

A side-by-side comparison with the change, usually as both absolute numbers and percentages.

Step 4 — Ask "why"

This is where Ana goes beyond a dashboard. Dashboards show you what; Ana can dig into why.

TYPE THIS TO ANA
The biggest change in that comparison — what's driving it? Dig one level deeper and tell me what stands out.
What you'll see

Ana investigates — splitting the change by product, customer segment, or whatever's relevant — and comes back with a short narrative: “Most of the increase comes from two enterprise accounts that ramped up in Q2…”

Course-correct freely

Said the wrong thing? Just say so: “Actually, use calendar year, not fiscal year” or “I meant units sold, not revenue.” Ana adjusts without starting over.

When to start a new thread

Stay in one thread while you're digging into one topic. Start a new chat when you switch topics — it keeps your history tidy and your answers focused.

Module 3 · Charts & Visuals

⏱️ 10 minutes🎯 Goal: turn answers into presentation-ready visuals

Any answer can become a chart — just ask. Ana often charts things automatically when it helps, but you're in control of the format.

Step 1 — Ask for a trend

TYPE THIS TO ANA
Show me that as a monthly trend for the last 12 months, as a line chart.

Step 2 — Change the visual

Don't like the format? Say so in plain English:

TYPE THIS TO ANA
Make that a bar chart instead, sorted from largest to smallest, and label the values.

Step 3 — Build a "one-slide" view

TYPE THIS TO ANA
Put this together as one summary I could paste into a slide: the headline number, the trend chart, and 3 bullet takeaways in plain English.
What you'll see

A tidy package — number, chart, and takeaways — ready to drop into your Monday meeting deck.

Getting visuals out

Charts and tables can be downloaded (chart as an image, table as a spreadsheet file) using the controls on the result — or just share the whole thread, which you'll do in Module 5.

Module 4 · Can I Trust This Number?

⏱️ 10 minutes🎯 Goal: know how to verify any answer — without reading code

This is the question every business user should ask — and the reason TextQL is built the way it is. You never have to take a number on faith. Three things protect you:

1 · Ana shows her work

Every answer comes with the full trail: the steps Ana took, the data she used, and the exact query she ran. You don't need to read the technical parts — the point is that someone can. If a number ever gets challenged in a meeting, the receipt exists.

TYPE THIS TO ANA
How did you calculate that? Explain it step by step in plain English.
What you'll see

A plain-English explanation: which data, which time window, what was included and excluded, and any definitions applied.

2 · Governed definitions — the company dictionary

Remember the ontology from “Meet Ana”? When your question touches an official metric, Ana uses the governed definition — the version your data team has reviewed and approved — not an improvised one. Try asking:

TYPE THIS TO ANA
What definition of revenue did you use? Is that the official, governed definition — and what does it include and exclude?
What you'll see

The definition spelled out — e.g. “invoiced revenue net of refunds, recognized on invoice date” — and where it comes from. This is why your number matches finance's number.

3 · You can challenge an answer

Healthy skepticism is part of the workflow. If something looks off, push back:

TYPE THIS TO ANA
That looks higher than I expected. Double-check it: is anything double-counted, and does the time window match what I asked for?
When Ana says the data isn't there

Sometimes the honest answer is “that isn't in the connected data.” That's a feature — Ana tells you instead of inventing something. Note what you wanted; it's exactly the feedback your data team needs to expand coverage.

Module 5 · Save & Share Your Work

⏱️ 10 minutes🎯 Goal: make your analysis useful to other people

An answer only you saw has limited value. Everything you've built in this workshop is already saved — now make it shareable.

  1. Name your thread. Rename this conversation to something findable — e.g. “Q2 regional sales review.” Future-you will thank present-you.
  2. Share the thread. Use the share option to send the conversation to a colleague. They see the full chain — questions, answers, charts, and the verification trail — not just a screenshot of a number.
  3. Export what you need. Download a table as a spreadsheet or a chart as an image for decks and docs.
  4. Make it repeatable. For a question you'll ask every week, ask Ana to package it:
TYPE THIS TO ANA
I'll want this same view every Monday. Save this analysis as a report I can re-run, and tell me how to get it refreshed regularly.
What you'll see

Ana sets up a re-runnable version of your analysis. Depending on how your workspace is configured, recurring delivery (for example to email or Slack) may be available — Ana will tell you what's enabled for your team.

Sharing > screenshotting

A shared thread keeps the context and the “show your work” trail attached to the number. A screenshot strips all of that away. When a decision rides on the number, share the thread.

Module 6 · Asking Great Questions

⏱️ 10 minutes🎯 Goal: the patterns that get great answers, every time

You don't need to be precise — Ana will ask when she needs clarity — but a little structure gets you to the answer faster. A strong question usually names three things:

📏 a metric  +  🗓️ a time period  +  🧩 a grouping or filter

“What was revenue (metric) last quarter (time), by product line (grouping)?”

Vague → better

Instead of…Try…
“sales numbers”“What was total revenue last month, and how does it compare to the month before?”
“customer info”“How many active customers do we have today, and how many did we add this quarter?”
“how is marketing doing”“Which marketing channel drove the most new customers this quarter, and at what cost per customer?”
“show me problems”“Which 5 products had the biggest drop in sales vs. last quarter?”

Power moves

  • Discover first: “What data do you have about ___?” before diving in.
  • Anchor to a decision: “I'm deciding whether to ___ — what should I look at?” Ana proposes the analysis.
  • Ask for the 'so what': end with “…and give me 3 takeaways in plain English.”
  • Set a recurring rhythm: for anything you ask weekly, save it as a report (Module 5).
  • Verify when stakes are high: “How did you calculate that?” (Module 4).

FAQ & Troubleshooting

Ana asked me a clarifying question. What do I do?

Just answer it in plain English. This is Ana being careful instead of guessing — pick the meaning you intended (“invoiced revenue”) and she'll continue.

The answer doesn't match a number I've seen elsewhere.

This is usually a definition difference, not an error — e.g. calendar vs. fiscal quarter, gross vs. net. Ask: “What definition and time window did you use? Why might this differ from [the other report]?” Ana will reconcile them. If they truly conflict, flag it to your data team — finding these is valuable.

Ana says she doesn't have the data I asked about.

The data source may not be connected yet, or you may not have access to it. Note exactly what you asked and send it to your data team or TextQL contact — requests like this drive what gets connected next.

My question is taking a while.

Bigger questions (long time ranges, many breakdowns) take longer — Ana narrates what she's doing in the meantime. For a faster first pass, narrow the time window and broaden later.

Can I ask about data in a document or spreadsheet I have?

Often yes — many workspaces let you upload a file into the chat for Ana to analyze alongside your connected data. Try attaching it, or ask your TextQL contact what's enabled.

Who do I contact for help?

Your internal workspace administrator for access issues; your TextQL team (FDE/account team) for anything else. If you're in a guided session, just ask your facilitator.
